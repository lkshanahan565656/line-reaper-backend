No-vig fair odds

A book’s two prices add up to more than 100%. Taking that margin out gives the fair chance of each side, the number every EV figure is measured against.

The margin is in the price

At −110 on both sides of a coin flip, each side implies 52.38%. Together that is 104.76%: the extra 4.76% is the book’s margin (the vig or hold). With it taken out, each side is 50%, or +100: that is the fair price.

Four ways to take it out

On a favourite against an underdog the margin is not charged evenly, and the methods split it differently. Here is Pinnacle-style −150 / +130 (the two sides add to 103.48%):

MethodFavouriteFairUnderdogFair
Power58.40%-14041.60%+140
Multiplicative57.98%-13842.02%+138
Additive58.26%-14041.74%+140
Shin58.26%-14041.74%+140
  • Multiplicative scales both chances down by the same share.
  • Additive takes the same number of points off each side.
  • Power raises each chance to the power that makes them add to 100%. It puts more of the margin on the longshot, which is how sharp books actually price (the favourite-longshot bias). It is the app’s default.
  • Shin models a share of the money as insiders. On a two-way market it works out to the same split as additive; on three-way markets (soccer) it differs.

The method changes a fair by a fraction of a point on most lines and by more on big favourites. The DEVIG picker in the ODDS SCREEN’s shopper modes switches every fair and EV on the page between the four.

Whose price is the fair?

A fair is only as good as the market it comes from. Line Reaper uses Pinnacle, the sharpest book that publishes prices, with Matchbook blended in when it prices the same line. When neither prices a market, the shopper uses the median no-vig of four or more books and labels it as a consensus. Pinnacle and Matchbook are shown as reference columns, never as a place to bet.

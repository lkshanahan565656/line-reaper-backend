Get help now (US)

  • National Problem Gambling Helpline: 1-800-GAMBLER (1-800-426-2537). Call or text, 24 hours a day, free and confidential. Run by the National Council on Problem Gambling, with online chat at ncpgambling.org.
  • Gamblers Anonymous: meetings in person and online at gamblersanonymous.org.
  • Gam-Anon, for family and friends of someone with a gambling problem: gam-anon.org.
  • 988 Suicide and Crisis Lifeline: call or text 988 if you are in crisis or thinking about hurting yourself.

Habits that keep betting under control

  • Set a bankroll you can afford to lose, separate from bill and savings money, and never top it up to chase losses.
  • Bet in fixed units. Line Reaper shows units so stakes stay small and steady.
  • Decide on time and money limits before you start, and use the deposit, loss and time limits your sportsbook or exchange offers.
  • Do not bet when you are upset, under pressure, or have been drinking.
  • Never borrow to bet, and never treat betting as a way to make a living or get out of debt.
  • Take regular breaks, and keep other hobbies and people in your life.

Warning signs

It may be time to get help if you:

  • bet more than you planned, or more than you can afford;
  • chase losses, or feel you need to bet more to get the same excitement;
  • hide your betting, or lie about it to people close to you;
  • borrow money, sell things or miss bills because of betting;
  • feel anxious, irritable or low when you are not betting; or
  • keep trying to cut back and cannot.

Blocking and self-exclusion

  • Self-exclusion: most US states with legal betting run a self-exclusion list through their gaming regulator, and every licensed sportsbook and exchange lets you close or limit your own account.
  • Blocking software: tools such as Gamban and BetBlocker block gambling sites and apps on your devices.
  • Bank blocks: many banks and card issuers let you block gambling transactions.
